Clearing MxYE at cycle 15 catches the chip mid-decision over whether MCBASE should take MC or keep its own value, and it takes a bit-interleaved mixture of both instead. Every offset therefore has two successors — the ordinary +3 and the crunched one — and a loop through that graph is a sprite of exactly that many lines.

MCBASE = ((MC | MCBASE) & 0x15) | ((MC & MCBASE) & 0x2a)   // MC = (MCBASE + 3) mod 64
Cycle detail

Built from Linus Åkesson's VIC 6569/8565 timing chart and Christian Bauer's The MOS 6567/6569 video controller (VIC-II) and its application in the Commodore 64 (1996). Cycle-exact border and register timings are documented for the 6569; the NTSC parts shift the idle cycles and sprite 0–2 fetches, which is reflected above, but treat their border cycles as approximate.

*The border-unit cycle numbers (17/18 and 56/57) are documented for the 6569 — I show them for the NTSC parts too, but treat those as approximate. And Bauer places the vertical border flip-flop's Y comparison in the last cycle of the line, while Åkesson's chart also marks a V-flag latch at 56/57; I've gone with Bauer since that's what emulators implement, but if you're doing something delicate in the upper/lower border it's worth testing on hardware.
